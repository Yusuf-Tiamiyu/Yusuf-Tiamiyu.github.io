Reorder Tracker

A smart Excel system that automatically monitors 1,578 laboratory items across every category — from gloves and reagents to analyser consumables — flagging anything that hits the 2-month stock threshold before it becomes a crisis.

Laboratory stockouts are dangerous — a missing reagent can delay diagnoses and affect patient care. But managing 1,578 items manually is impossible. This tracker was built to make it automatic: set up each item once, and the system tells you every day whether you’re safe or need to act.

The core logic is simple but powerful. Every item has a Reorder Level set to 2 months of average usage. When Current Qty drops to or below that level, the Status column instantly flips from green “OK” to red “REORDER NOW” — no manual checking, no formulas you have to recalculate. It just works.

The file also tracks issuances (every item going out gets logged in Sheet 2), and Sheet 3 auto-generates a monthly summary for any month you specify — showing total consumption per item, number of transactions, and who items were issued to most.

The tracker was intentionally built so any team member can use it — no Excel expertise required. Update the quantity, and everything else is automatic.

01

The 2-Month Rule

Every item’s Reorder Level is set to exactly 2 months of average monthly usage. So when the tracker flags REORDER NOW, you still have 2 full months of stock to place an order, wait for delivery, and receive the goods. This prevents stockouts even with slow procurement cycles.

02

Automatic Status

The Status column runs a simple IF formula. You never type OK or REORDER NOW — it calculates itself every time the spreadsheet opens or a quantity changes. Green = safe. Red = act now.

03

Months of Stock

A calculated column shows exactly how many months of stock remain based on current quantity and average usage. This is more useful than just OK/REORDER — it tells you if you have 0.5 months or 12 months, so you can prioritise your orders.

Status column — auto-calculates
-- Status: compares current qty against reorder level
=IF(C2 <= D2, "REORDER NOW", "OK")
Avg monthly usage — auto-derived from reorder level
-- Avg monthly usage = Reorder Level / 2 (since level = 2 months of usage)
=D2 / 2
Months of stock remaining — auto-calculates
-- Months of stock = Current Qty / Avg Monthly Usage
=IFERROR(C2 / H2, 0)
-- IFERROR handles items with 0 monthly usage (no divide-by-zero errors)
Monthly summary — dynamic by month selection
-- Total issued for an item in the selected month
=SUMIFS(IssuanceLog[Qty Issued],
         IssuanceLog[Item ID], A7,
         IssuanceLog[Month],   $B$4)
-- Change B4 (the month selector) and the whole summary updates instantly

When this tracker was first deployed and populated, the findings were stark. Here’s what the data revealed.
🚨
89.5% of items were below reorder level. Of 1,578 tracked items, 1,413 were flagged REORDER NOW on day one. This was invisible without the tracker — nothing was being automatically monitored before it was built.
⚠
~55% had zero stock. Approximately 870 items had a Current Qty of 0 — meaning they were completely out of stock with no buffer whatsoever. For critical lab consumables like collection tubes and reagents, this is a patient safety risk.
📈
A handful of items had excessive stock. Items like Stool Containers (3,850 units, 38.5 months), Urine Containers (5,025 units), and Barcode Labels (18,000 units) had far more stock than needed — capital tied up that could be better used elsewhere.
✔
Only 165 items were genuinely healthy. These included PPE (gloves, waste bags, lab coats), some collection tubes, and a few key reagents. Even these needed ongoing monitoring to stay above threshold.
🌟
The biggest value: the tracker made the invisible visible. Before this tool, the only way to know stock levels was to physically check or ask someone. Now, anyone can open the file and see the full picture in seconds — and the system tells you exactly what to order and when.

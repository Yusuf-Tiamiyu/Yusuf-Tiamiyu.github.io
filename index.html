I joined Cerba Lancet Nigeria as a data analyst in December 2024. Since July 2025 I’ve also been its Data Protection Officer, so part of my week is analysis and part is data protection: compliance audits across our sites, breach procedures, and training staff. The two jobs overlap, because most privacy questions are questions about where data goes and who can see it.

Most of my analysis is about turnaround time, the gap between a sample being booked in and its result being verified. When I started, the weekly TAT report took someone a full day in Excel and arrived a week late. I replaced it with a pipeline and a dashboard that supervisors now use on their daily calls. I also built the inventory tracker the labs use to reorder supplies, and the weekly operations dashboard managers use to compare sites.

I studied chemistry at Lagos State University. My final-year project, on groundwater quality across 25 wells, became a paper in Hydrology in 2023. Lab work taught me not to trust a result until I know how it was produced, which is most of data cleaning too.

Before analytics I worked in quality assurance at Flour Mills of Nigeria, where I noticed that shifts staffed only by new hires had the biggest weight deviations. The supervisor changed the roster so every shift had someone experienced on it. Outside work, I play a lot of video games.

How I work

How I work

01
Start from the decision

Before I open the data I ask who will act on the answer and what they would do differently. For the TAT dashboard, that meant building it around what supervisors check on their daily operational calls.

02
Check the data before using it

I count rows against the source, look for duplicates and impossible values, and write the checks down so they run again next time. In the COVID project, Hong Kong was missing 316 days, which a simple group-by would never have shown.

03
Pick definitions I can defend

A test with no verification time is left out, not counted as a failure. Rates are calculated once, from counts. Choices like these decide whether two managers looking at the same week see the same number.

04
End with what to do

“Staff these two sites properly and about 139 breaches a week go away” is something a manager can plan around. I try to finish every analysis with a recommendation and an estimate of what it’s worth.
